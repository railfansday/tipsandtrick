I love how paper bag snowflakes are an inexpensive handmade decor item and they are such a fun way to decorate for the holiday season! A few years ago, I bought paper snowflakes to put up in my dining area and paid almost $12 for a set of three. To make this hand-crafted version of these natural colored paper snowflakes were $1.98 from Walmart and I had bags left over for school lunches! a 50 bag pack will make about 3 large paper snowflakes. You could even make GIANT snowflakes with grocery brown paper bags.

According to recent reports, to start making your paper bag snowflakes, lay the first bag flat on a work table surface to start to layer the bags. Run a bead of either hot glue or tacky white craft glue down the center of the bag and at the bottom of the bag at the base where it would stand up at the folded bottom (white craft glue is the best option if the kids are doing the project), creating an upside down T shape with the glue.

Lay the second bag on top and repeat the process for the next paper bag as well. Continue laying the next bag on top of each other going in the same direction until you have a stack of about 12- 15 bags glued together.

In a recent development, at this point in your paper bag snowflake making, if you are using white craft glue you will have to wait until the glue dries. If you are using hot glue, you can continue on to the next step of cutting your snowflake pattern. If the glue isn’t holding a well as you would like, use a stapler on the edges to staple together near the bottom or use clear tape.

It has been revealed that this next step is where you are going to make your snowflake pattern by using your scissors to cute to create different patterns and different shapes at the open end of the bag. I found when creating my paper stars to use a marker to draw my shapes on ahead of time It’s key to leave some intact places at the sides of the paper bags so they don’t come a part when “unfolding”.

According to recent reports, when making patterns on your snowflake DIYs, try varying the cuts. To make a more pointy snowflake, cut the top of the bag to a point,and a gentler curve to make it more rounded. Try cutting large and small shapes out in the sides, always leaving a little bit of the edges of the bag attached. This is where strong and sharp scissors come in to cut though the bags. Don’t try to cut through the entire thing at once and power through it. Cut a manageable amount at a time, even if it’s a bag at a time. Always use caution when using sharp scissors.

I did find sometimes the bags together were too thick, and I had to cut through the side of the bags a few at a time instead of tackling the entire stack at once to get good cuts and shapes.

Once your paper bags are cut, grab the top of the first bag and the top of the last bag and gently open up your paper bag stars. The top edges should open until they touch each other. At this point you want to either staple, tape, or paper clip them together to hold the star in place.

While I made brown paper bag snowflakes, it’s pretty to mix white paper bag snowflakes in with them. You can even use a hole punch to make different designs and try large and small angle cuts. These can be hung from the ceiling with yarn or twine. Command hooks or removable hooks work well since they are seasonal decor.

According to recent reports, if you cut your bags down and glue the open edges, just make sure that all of the edges are lined up the same way, otherwise the DIY paper bag snowflakes won’t open correctly. To store for next year, just snip the tape on the ends where they meet and fold up to put away.
