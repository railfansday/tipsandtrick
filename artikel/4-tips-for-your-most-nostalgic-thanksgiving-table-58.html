“Regal” would be the word we’re going for this Thanksgiving. We found these Spode Thanksgiving plates at Wayfair, and you just know the girls will call dibs. “I want the fox!” “I want the horse!” Someone will forever be the hound dog. It’s going to be a core memory (we hope!). To set the table, we grabbed some woven rattan chargers to make the plates pop without clashing, and a napkin layered between the dinner and salad plates turned everything a bit dressy. Is the dress code formal this year? It just might be!

It has been revealed that the dishes are the stars (both meanings apply — grab Chris’s recipes here), so everything else plays a supporting role, except the brass bird. You don’t need fresh everything for Thanksgiving every year, but it’s good to have one thing that keeps coming back. He’s the one that’ll get saved. Pardoned. (Sorry. Had to.). The dinnerware set comes with dinner, salad, and bread plates, and none of them scream Thanksgiving. Turkey salad plates were an option, but we went with one of every animal instead, about 20 in all, with no repeats. There are pheasants, rabbits, a hound dog, a horse, and a fox. It’s an instant conversation starter (“Oh, you’re at the fox seat”), and that kind of detail is exactly what nostalgia is made of.

Recent findings suggest that the rattan chargers were on our summer table. The silverware is our everyday set, and a great goblet works in any season. The splurge was the paisley linen napkins. They were bought for the occasion, but they’ll show up at every special dinner, which makes them a better buy than holiday napkins that sit in a bin 11 months a year. One simple trick: a napkin wrapped around a bottle of sparkling cider makes it feel like fine dining.

According to recent reports, when guests arrive, the first thing they’ll ask is where to sit. “Wherever you want” just leaves them feeling conflicted. Think about who’s coming and who will have the best conversations, then give everyone a spot. Sometimes you just have to lean into the theme with turkey place card holders, each finished with a ribboned gift tag and handwritten names. Bonus: guests can take the tags home, and they’re a sweet nod to the holidays ahead.

Recent findings suggest that christmas and Halloween both have decorations that come out year after year, and Thanksgiving deserves one too. Lots of families serve Thanksgiving dinner buffet-style, which leaves room on the table for a statement piece. Our brass bird is one of the only Thanksgiving-specific pieces here, so spending a little more on him is easy to justify. (Thinking about what we spend on a nice Christmas tree as an example!).

In a recent development, this is the easiest, most budget-friendly update there is. Change your tapers and ribbon for each holiday and keep everything else the same. These Wayfair tapers are in the color Portobello, a brown that ties all the tones into the royal blue tablecloth. They’re dripless, they burn for a long time (which you want for a long dinner), and they come in lots of colors. Next year, a upcoming tablecloth and a new taper color will give the same table a whole new look.
