I finally have a tiny bit of brain space (and I do mean tiny) to start back into the guest cottage design process, namely the bathroom that has been at a standstill awaiting my flooring decision. I had it all locked in- thin-set brick in a herringbone pattern. I sampled a ton, made my decision, and ordered a box. I was about to order the rest, but as the wood on the walls went in, I realized that this bathroom is going to lean VERY rustic, with a lot of dark reddish tones on all walls, ceilings, AND the floor. And while I like rustic in theory, and nothing is wrong with leaning into a vibe, I had second thoughts that turned into a U-turn. Are we missing an opportunity to do something spectacular and extra beautiful??? I know that the brick will look good, but could something else look even better?

Sources indicate that I wrote about wanting a higher-gloss tile finish to contrast against all the rustic texture a couple of weeks ago when two commenters warned me not to do it. And I listened. One said they were in the middle of tearing out their high-gloss tile as we speak. OOF. Now I have a feeling the tile is a large-scale 12×24 porcelain situation that a lot of builders put in (because it’s affordable to buy and install) and it’s 100% too slippery for a floor. When I took to Reddit, this was the case – all significant-format tile complaints. Sure, the polished high-gloss doesn’t help, but I wonder if it’s more the lack of texture on the tile or grout lines that makes it specifically slippery. Ceramic tile doesn’t come that big, but if it did, I wonder if it would be almost as slippery. So let’s go through the pros and cons of bathroom tile flooring, what we are considering, and then I’d LOVE to hear any warnings about high-gloss tile. Recent findings suggest that yes! Tiles have a DCOF (Dynamic Coefficient of Friction) rating that corresponds to slip risk. Depending on the tile’s DCOF there are recommended uses. For interior wet spaces you want to find a tile that has a wet DCOF of at least 0.42. Most tile manufacturers will also divide their tile offerings based on recommended use.
