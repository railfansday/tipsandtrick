Ceramic Christmas villages are a sweet addition to holiday decorating, they are also easy to find at thrift stores. Some times you can find them, but they don’t match, If you’re looking for a way to create a colorful and eclectic Christmas color palette , try using rainbow colors to create a painted ceramic Christmas village houses with fun and bright paints. For even more fun, use multi-purpose paint with a little sparkle in it. Old ceramic houses can be found for a few dollars each, and same for the paint. with 7 houses, this project can be expected to cost under $20, depending on how much the houses cost.

Sources indicate that in the 1980’s and 90’s, it seemed like a lot of households had these. We had a neighbor that had 30 or 40, and would nestle her entire collection in faux snow under her tree. She also had a cement goose that sat on her front stoop that was super cute. She would dress it up for every holiday, including a bright yellow raincoat for rainy days… but that is an entirely other another story all together. Let’s get back to the ceramic houses. These houses aren’t actually from the same collection. I picked them up here and there at different visits to the thrift store. Some of them are even meant for a tealight. Since they are now painted, I will probably put a battery operated candle inside, or fairy lights. I started by  giving them a dusting and cleaning and removing some candle wax. Regular craft paint can be used, They aren’t going to get heavy use, so I’m not worried about chipping . Also, once these are painted only use fairy lights or battery operated candles in them. Even if they are made for it, don’t use real candles any more. (Related: Industry News: My “Super Plush” Bathroom Gem Made It on...)

Sources indicate that -Multi-purpose paint in a base color and a sparkle color or paintable glitter paint. We used DecoArt multi-surface and Glamour Dust paint. (Related: Tech Report: Pink Paint Refreshed a Kitchen’s “Generic”...)

-Clear coat sealer -if using regular acrylic craft paint.

-Primer or base coat of white paint.

-Tarp or covering for the table.

Started by putting on a layer of base coat of the multi-surface craft paint. on the houses and let dry. This may take 1 -2 coats for complete coverage. Let dry completely before moving onto the next step.

It has been revealed that even though you can paint Glamour Dust Paint on it’s own, it’s really much more intense with a paint color underneath. By mixing and matching paints, you can make some really fun color combinations. I matched my acrylic paint as close as I could to my Glamour Dust paint to get a matte solid finish and cover up the detail on the house underneath. Let your acrylic paint dry completely before applying a layer of Glamour Dust. There’s a chart farther down in the post you can pin and save for later.

Recent findings suggest that it’s kind of fun how painting something with acrylic paint makes it look different, like these trophies I painted a few years ago.The Glamour Dust is fun because it has a really fine glitter, so you get more of a matte, sparkly finish which is really pretty. It works best when applying thin coats, and letting them dry to build up a more glittery finish.

I ended up with about 2 coats of acrylic paint, and 5 coats of Glamour Dust on each house by the time I was done.

I used a craft foam brush in the beginning, and then I switched to a regular soft bristled brush. It worked much better to get in the cracks and crevices.

Once they dried, they had such a pretty shimmer and sparkle.

It was a fun to turn the village into a rainbow holiday decor!

Industry observers note that up close you can really see the shimmer of the Glamour Dust. It’s so pretty in person!
