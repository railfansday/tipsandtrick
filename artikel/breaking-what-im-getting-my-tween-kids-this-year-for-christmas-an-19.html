We had a fun review-style post for you today, but had to bump it, so I figured it might be fun to show you what I’m getting my kids this year (as of now). I’m ahead of the game because of my gift guide research – it’s not normal to be so on top of it, I promise, but man, it feels good to have them locked down. My kids don’t read the blog, don’t worry. The only reason I’m sharing is because I think I have some good ideas that could be fun for you to copy. As a reminder, my tween kids are 11 (Elliot, girl) and 13 (Charlie, boy), which means they are in the middle of wanting to be perceived as teenagers, but their brains and bodies aren’t there yet. So obviously they beg for things like iPhones and AirPods, crop tops and makeup, but are sooooo happy to get squishies and cozy faux fur blankets. It’s so sweet and confusing, and while the pushing back can be unrelenting, they are so stinking fun to be around, so I’ll take it! We obsess about making the gifts equal (both in amount and optics), and Brian is major on each having something visually fun to walk into the room on Christmas morning to surprise them, so we are still figuring out that (I’m leaning towards a fun furry bean bag for their rooms and doing the research now between Love Sac and Cordaroys, so stay tuned. I might get one of each so I can do a proper review). I’m also behind on getting matching family pajamas, and so many are sold out now, so we’ll see….

Industry observers note that LED lights for his room. I didn’t know the level of tween and teen obsession over LED lights until after my tween gift guide, so I’ve since added these (which are better for walls but more expensive than the strip lights). But THEY ARE OBSESSED. I did a lot of research, and these are the good ones on a track that look better on walls and won’t rip off paint when taking them off (and have a cord cover that reads as square and white). But if that’s too much of a commitment (and work to put them up), I also think this lamp is a great alternative: less work, less money, and can go anywhere (getting Elliot it for her room). We are installing them around the ceiling, but you could just do under shelves or in random strips on the walls. They can turn on and “play” to music and obviously change a ton of colors. Oh, and yes, they work with phone apps, but they can also just be manual since our kids don’t have phones yet. (Related: Tech Report: This “Strange” Bathroom’s Reno Is a Must-S...)

Sources indicate that dungeon Carl Crawl Ornament: This kid has devoured this book series this year, so for his special ornament I ordered this (it was more $$ than it should be, but it’s an Etsy maker, so I felt ok about it). And while I know some of these books aren’t totally age-appropriate, we’ve chosen to stay silent with an “as long as he’s reading” attitude. That’s not universal for any reading, obviously; I just know that this series can be dark and violent, but it’s fiction and entertaining as hell, so we are ok with it. (Related: Designers Say These 5 Items Add Polish Into Any Living ...)

Custom Photo Playing Cards: Charlie is really into magic tricks (he’s so good!) and poker, so in the name of “meeting him where he’s at,” I thought that getting custom playing cards with pictures of him and his friends and pups/pigs would be fun (I snuck in a couple of photos of the family, but I didn’t want to be cringe, so it’s mostly non-family photos). I had my iPhone search his face, then I dragged and dropped favorites into a quick album (you can do just a few photos repeated or choose 52 different photos). I uploaded it to WeTransfer, but I guess a lot of them were Live photos, so I had to redo a lot of them (by turning them from “live” into “still”), so it did take a couple hours all in all. I haven’t received these yet, but I think he’s going to like them.

For a bigger present, we are debating getting him a monitor/keyboard and mouse (which I guess is just a screen) to attach to our video game console so he can play more comfortably. I have two leftover Mac monitors from when we had an office, so I’m thinking we’ll hand those down, but those have internet, and a simple monitor is just a screen. He’s begging for it. While I don’t really want to lean harder into video games (like at all), he says that it’s just easier to sit and play rather than lie on the guest bed with a massive TV, and buying the three is around $150, so it’s not as intense or expensive as I had thought (but again, I might hand him down our old Apple monitor, which looks even fancier but is free to us). I feel very uncomfortable in this whole world TBH (both knowledge of how it works and because I’m riddled with parenting hesitation), so we’ll see. If so, it would be in the guest bedroom on the desk or in the landing. Send help. Especially if you made it through this phase!! Even my mom (who raised 6 kids), who is helping raise my nephews, says that it’s so much harder now due to the screen options these kids have. This is obviously a much larger conversation, but I know that a lot of you will connect to the struggle.

Sources indicate that scrapbook Making Kit: Selfishly, I just want a scrapbook buddy, but when I pitched the idea to her, she LOVED IT and wanted it. While I don’t usually put all these bells and whistles on mine, I know it will make it more fun for her. I’ve also just got this photo printer to help with the fun (unsure if this will be a gift for her or a family gift since she doesn’t have a phone yet).

Sources indicate that LED Lamp: I know that she will want the LED lights, but I’m hoping that this will suffice for her room since she has beadboard (which is fine, but still). It’s certainly less of a setup, but it still gives off that look (and such a minimal vibe). Comes in a few colors, and I’m getting her the one in “champagne”.

Viral Double-Tipped Really Good Markers: She doesn’t know about these yet, but I hear that they are really, really good, and she still uses her favorite markers from a few years ago, but has lost a lot, so this is a great upgrade. I’m also getting her that watercolor book (she has a paint kit she loves already).

According to recent reports, three-month subscription to Baketivity: Every Monday, Elliot and one of her best friends bake at our house, and they have gotten so into it that I know that having at least one of these a month will hopefully grow their skills (she really wants to be a baker right now, along with a writer, dancer, and artist:)). They already do it completely independently, but they are begging for more tools and recipes and just fun bells and whistles. So we’ll start with the first three months (arrives one at a time), and if she is still really into it. They have this baking academy which is more intense and expensive, but I’m open to it if she’s serious about learning how to bake (almost like you’d invest in a sport; I believe in investing in creativity). Last year we got them both Crunchlab subscriptions, and she LOVES them (Charlie was hit or miss). So this is the baking version of it.

Recent findings suggest that for her bigger present, she is begging for Uggs, so we’ll see about that! I just know they’ll get so trashed up here, and her feet grow so fast, so we’ve remarked no every other time she’s asked, but maybe we can Scotchgard them? But then do Uggs and a monitor feel equal??? I don’t know!! Thus the dual bean bag conversation.

Recent findings suggest that bambuu 3D Printer: They campaigned hard for this, so they are going to be psyched (but the whole family is sharing it). Our kids have played with the Toybox 3D printer for 5 years, and it’s been awesome, but they are outgrowing its capabilities. Apparently, you can make everything on this printer (Brian is just as excited). I’m thinking it might be a great way to engage our kids in coming up with some home solutions (like maybe I’ll tie it to their allowance and “pay” them to make drawer dividers or a holder for my steamer in the closet, etc.). Brian did all the research here.

Recent findings suggest that custom Pet Dish: UGH. This turned out so stinking cute I can’t believe it. I sent in a photo of our pups and their names, and two weeks later I got this! I was just going to get one for Elliot, but I knew that Charlie would want one, too, so I got one for each of them.

Custom Mixed Records from this year of their life. I’m so excited about this. I created a playlist on Spotify from what I hear them listening to (Elliot did her own on Spotify, not knowing this is what it is for). You can choose up to 14 songs and order them just like you would a CD from back in the day. Both our kids have record players (Christmas last year or the year before), and we have one out in the game room, so my hope is to do this every year, and then when they are in their 20s and 30s, they’ll get all nostalgic while listening to them. I mean, I hope they listen to them now, but it’s such a great time capsule (I wish to GOD I had saved all of my CD books from college and my early 20s). Brian also might be getting one of these to celebrate our 20th anniversary 🙂 Also, you can choose the cover art, so I’ll likely choose a photo of them and their friends from this year.

Sources indicate that listen, I don’t want to yuck your yum if you have already bought this, but I seriously regret buying Charlie his electric scooter a couple of Christmases ago. Once they have an E-scooter, a normal bike is so boring and slow to them. Not to mention how dangerous the E-bikes can be (his scooter can’t go above 15 mph, but still). It was a massive mistake, and I pray that it will break down all the time and we will not replace it. Kids should be pedaling bikes with their feet and no motor until they are old enough to drive and know the road rules, full stop. I will say we have strict rules about where he can go (only in our few-block quadrant that is super low-traffic) and I’m glad it has a speed maximum. I’m not a monster; I’m not going to intentionally break it, but yes, I am hoping it breaks down. Even if they beg, think twice! Instead, I’d get a razor, a drifter, or go-karts. Those are still really fun, and you can go fast, but it’s more for parking lots or sports courts, so inherently less dangerous than on a street.

Ok that’s it. I’m still going to shop from our Tween shop page and get them some of the Touchland sanitizers, a bedazzling kit, and sweatshirts, too. Now starts the joy of making sure they both have the same amount and that everything feels even and fair.

I wish us all luck!
