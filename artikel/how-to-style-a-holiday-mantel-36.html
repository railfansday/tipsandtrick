How to Style a Holiday Mantel

Industry observers note that we decorate our entire house for Christmas, but the living room is really the star of the show. It’s where the big tree goes, where our girls open their presents on Christmas morning, and where we entertain guests over the holidays. While the tree commands a lot of attention, the true focal point of the room is the fireplace. It’s the obvious spot for the stockings, but it’s also a great canvas for adding greenery and other styling elements to make the room feel like the holidays. Here’s the formula we come back to every year, plus a look back at our last three mantels.

It has been revealed that we’ve styled our mantel in different ways over the years and have landed on a formula for foolproof mantel styling. Here’s how it comes together.

We always start with faux greenery, and our go-to is cedar — we love the airy look of the branches and the texture of the needles. Whatever garland you use, make sure it’s longer than your mantel so you have room for the swags and some to hang off each side. Some people drape garland straight across the mantel, but we like to let it swag down on both sides. This way, the garland stays visible and you leave room for other styling elements on the surface of the mantel. (Related: Design SOS: 2 Reader Dining Rooms (Finally) Finished—On...)

Next, add something to give your greenery interest. Maybe that’s more greenery in a different texture, like a mixed eucalyptus or pine. Other options include beaded garland, brass bells, dried orange slice garlands, or even something unique like the brass “Merry Christmas” banner we’ve used in the past. This is also the step where you’d add lights, if you want them. We typically leave lights out of our garland, preferring candlelight and other ambient light, but they’re a great option if you’re looking for some extra sparkle.

Sources indicate that it’s no secret that we love a holiday bow, and the mantel is no exception. Every styling moment needs a focal point, and we like to use a large bow in the center to pull everything together. A lot of our holiday decor gets reused and repurposed around the house year after year, so we change up the ribbon color to keep things feeling fresh.

If you’re not confident hand tying bows there are tons of pre-made tree topper options out there that would work beautifully on a mantle.

It’s easy to overdo the top of the mantel and take away from the moment you’ve created with the garland, but really all you need is a little height on one or both sides to balance the visual weight of the garland and stockings below. We like to use candlesticks for this, since they add height and ambiance in one move, but you can also use other things from around the house, like a vase full of greenery or even a piece of art on one side.

There are many ways to hang stockings, and it really depends on the shape and structure of your mantel. We use small screw-in hooks on the underside of ours. The holes are barely visible the rest of the year and they look cleaner than stocking holders. You can go all matching with your stockings or mix and match patterns (we designed a collection made just for this!). We have a large family, so we typically put three on one side and two on the other. Just remember to place something vertical on the mantel diagonally across from your stockings so the visual weight feels even on both sides.

In a recent development, last year, our Christmas tree was to the left of the fireplace, so we opted to go a little lighter on the garland to avoid the greenery blending in with the tree. We layered a red beaded garland over the top with a large burgundy ribbon in the center (full permission to mix your red tones — it looks so refined). We hung the stockings just below the garland so the beads stayed the focus.

According to recent reports, instead of layering in beads or metal, this year we mixed greenery of different textures, like cedar and Norfolk pine. The result is a lush, botanical moment that needs little else besides some red ribbon for contrast.

According to recent reports, this mantel plays with warm and cool tones — the silver ribbon layered with the brass “Merry Christmas” garland, the warm gold of the mirror contrasted with the cool-toned sage stockings underneath. A silver ribbon bow and tall tapers on both sides keep it feeling balanced and a little unexpected.

Sources indicate that you rarely need just one garland for a mantel. They look best layered, with multiple strands of the same type or a mix of greenery for a fuller, more botanical look. If you’re following our formula, place one garland on either side of the bow at the center point — just make sure the branches point outward, not back toward the center.

Sources indicate that we use Command hooks for ours — one in the center (where the bow usually goes) and one at each anchor point on the outer corners of the mantel. Because of the shape of our mantel, we hang our stockings from small screw-in hooks on the underside, but you can also use Command hooks or a weighted stocking holder like this one.
