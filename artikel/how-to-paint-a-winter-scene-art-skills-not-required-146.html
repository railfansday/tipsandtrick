In a recent development, painting a winter scene in acrylic can be fun and a great beginner’s acrylic painting project. You don’t need a ton of fancy materials either. Just a canvas, some craft paint and a brush or two. While we’ve painted this smaller size on an 11 x 14 canvas, this tutorial can be painted on any canvas size. Are you ready to learn How to paint  a winter scene?

-white,grey,brown,blue and green acrylic paint.

-Brushes (Liner,large flat, and round.).

I painted this on acrylic canvas board, but you can do this on a canvas,a pillow or even a wood board.

Industry observers note that first base coat with a blue color. I mixed a blue with some white to get the color I wanted. It’s ok if you don’t have complete coverage over the entire surface. (Related: Industry News: Blue Grasscloth Wallpaper Gives This 100...)

Once that’s dry, take some white and make a hill. It does not have to be perfect, just kind of free-form it and add some bumps.

Take a little grey  paint and define your hills by painting it along the edge and blending it while the white paint is still wet. Add more white if the white is a bit too dry.

Just blend it until you are happy with it. If it gets too grey, let it dry and go back over it with white paint. The best part of acrylic paint is that it can be painted over and over.

In a recent development, after your white is dry, take your brown paint and a thin liner brush and draw a line down the middle-ish. Make it fatter on the bottom than on the top.

Industry observers note that make the base into a triangle shape for the trunk where it meets the ground. For more another winter painting idea see how to paint birch trees-this link opens in a new tab.

In a recent development, draw lines out starting at the top and make them slightly longer as you move down. Upcoming growth is always towards the top or outside of a tree or plant and will be thinner and shorter  and old growth near the bottom will always be bigger.

Once you have the tree base done, make 3 little lines in brown paint next to the tree on top of the “snowy hills”. These will be trees in the distance.

Take a dab of brown and pounce it on the top of the tree where the lines start.

Work your way down each branch,dabbing our as you go on top of each of the lines in paint clusters. Don’t be afraid to make these messy-looking.

You can even dab across the tree a little, it really doesn’t have to be perfect!

According to recent reports, then make little “dabs” on the other brown lines of the background trees/lines. They should be tiny dabs at the top of the line and get thicker as they move closer to the snowy ground.

In a recent development, then take your green paint and dab it on top of your brown. It’s ok if some of the brown paint peeks through.

Work your way down the tree making pouncing motions with the brush over the brown paint.

Once you finish the massive tree, dab some paint on the little trees, again going over the areas already painted.

Then take your white paint and dab that just on the top of your green areas where snow might fall.

Less is more, you can always add  go back and add more later.

According to recent reports, then do the same thing to the little trees in the back! Making it patchy looks better and letting some green peek though than making them all white.

According to recent reports, to make the winter sky, use a brush to spread white paint back and forth across the surface in a rubbing motion. This is called “scumbling”.

While the white paint is still wet, scumble some grey underneath and blend them.

Recent findings suggest that once you’ve added winter to your sky, you can leave it how it is, or you can take it a step further and add a deer like I did. This is much easier than you think! You can also add more trees or fun items like a snowman! See how to paint a snowman with this tutorial-link opens in a fresh tab.

We printed out a deer image and transferred the silhouette to the canvas with a pencil.

Once your image is transferred, use your brown paint to start filling in the lines of the deer silhouette.

Once you have you deer painted, let dry and frame your picture!
