There is something about brass that has a timeless quality to it. This project made with air dry clay will have people wondering if these ornaments real metal! We used air dry clay, metallic paint and real pine branch sprigs  to create these faux brass botanical ornaments. They look almost like real metal and the plant impressions have a pretty natural look almost like they were molded or cast that way. This project can also be switched up by changing the paint finish to silver or even copper. This air dry clay ornament project will take about an hour of creative time to make the first part, overnight time for the clay to dry, and then about an hour of painting time to complete the rest.

-Dedicated clay rolling pin or dowel rod.

-Faux or real non-toxic pine plant branches or twigs.

-Antique Gold, Bronze or Brass metallic craft paint.

-Small stick or straw to make a hole in the ornament.

You can find the supplies here an Amazon for this project.

Here’s a tip when working with clay: use parchment paper under clay to make it easier to move and flip while drying. (Related: How To Make Your Next Year Better/Prettier With Good Ho...)

For my botanical impression, I used small sprigs from my Alaskan Cedar, and my Scotch Pine. Any evergreen or branch that has stiff and retains needles will work. I liked the Alaskan Cedar, because it has small berry-like pine cones that I knew would look cool. When using fresh greenery, make sure you always know what you are picking. Some evergreen look a likes can be toxic.

While the clay is still wet, press the branches into the clay. Rolling a dowel rod across the clay and branch helps to apply even pressure and create a nice, deep impression. After rolling, carefully lift the branches  straight up so the impression doesn’t smudge. Some pieces might get stuck in the clay. A toothpick can help pick out any little pieces without disturbing the impression. (Related: 10 Easy Pieces: Cloth Bread Bags...)

According to recent reports, let the clay disk dry a full 24-48 hours before painting. When the top had completely dried, I flipped it over the make sure the underside dried too. If you experience cracking on the surface, see this post about a clay wrapped vase that deals with air dry clay cracking. Once the clay is dry, paint with an antique gold paint in metallic acrylic to paint over the front, making sure to get in all of the pattern, including the sides. If the color isn’t deep enough, mix a little bronze into the gold. Once the paint is dry, flip over and paint the back. This may take 1-2 coats.

Once  the ornament is dry, hang on twine or a pretty ribbon. The easiest way is to cut the ribbon/twine to about 12″ long, thread through the hole and then knot together at the top for a 6″ length to hang. When you hang them, then let everyone guess if these faux brass botanical ornaments are real metal or not! Since they are air dry clay, they will be a little more delicate, and can creak if dropped.

You could also create these with letter stamps to make a monogrammed initial ornament, or create cute gift tags!
